# Revenue Model

**Status:** Hypothesis. No payment, pricing or partnership has been tested.

## What changed and why

The earlier model ("subscription + ads + data + partnerships") is replaced:

| Removed | Why |
|---|---|
| **Subscription** | The job is a one-off: most students search once, maybe twice, per degree. There is nothing to renew. |
| **Ads** | Ads compete with what we sell, which is trust. Paid placement next to "verified" listings makes students doubt the ranking. |
| **Selling data** | Directly conflicts with GDPR and with student trust. It would also make the legal review much harder. |

## Who has the pain vs. who can pay

- **Deadline Panic Settler:** has the most pain but the least money (€600–900/month budget). They also compare us with Idealista, which is free.
- **Prepared Insider:** can pay, but needs us less.
- **Universities, landlords, agencies and residences:** they all benefit when students find and sign housing early, and they have budgets.

So the student-facing core is **free**, and revenue comes mainly from the other parties.

## Revenue streams (in order of priority to test)

### 1. University partnerships (lead hypothesis)
- **Who pays:** IE first (Student Life, admissions or housing services), then other universities with large international intakes.
- **What they get:** a branded housing-discovery service for admitted students, aggregated data on how and when their students find housing (anonymised, with consent), fewer students arriving without housing.
- **Pricing to test:** an annual licence, or a fee per incoming student.
- **Why it's the lead:** one contract reaches a whole cohort and also solves distribution, and the student pays nothing.

### 2. Supply side: landlords, agencies, student residences
- **Verified listing fee:** landlords and agencies pay for Level 2 verification and the "Verified" badge (see `verification.md`).
- **Residence referral commission:** private student residences pay a commission per signed booking. This is already standard practice in that market.
- **Legal fit:** since Spain's 2023 Housing Law (Ley 12/2023), agency management fees for rentals are the landlord's cost, not the tenant's. A supply-side model fits that rule.

### 3. Optional paid "Settled" package for students or parents (test only)
- **What it includes:** a one-off concierge service with shortlisting, Level 4 in-person viewing, contract checklist review and help with landlord communication.
- **Who pays:** often parents, who fund and guarantee the rental.
- **Legal flag:** this must be clearly a separate service and not a disguised agency fee charged to the tenant. Spain's consumer ministry has warned agencies that charging tenants management fees is illegal. **Get this reviewed before charging anyone.**

## Unit economics to measure (no numbers yet)

- **Revenue per student** = (university fee per student) + (supply-side revenue per signed lease) + (package uptake × package price)
- **Cost per student** = acquisition cost + verification cost (minutes per Level 2 check × listings per student) + concierge time
- **Seasonality:** most revenue arrives in a few months before each intake (mainly summer). Cash planning has to account for this.

Measure these during the concierge pilot. Don't model them until we have real minutes and real conversion numbers.

## Validation tests

| Stream | Test | Strong evidence |
|---|---|---|
| University | Pitch IE Student Life / housing services with the problem data and the prototype | A signed letter of intent or pilot agreement (not just "interesting") |
| Verified listing fee | Offer verification to 10 landlords and agencies at a stated price | At least 3 pay, or commit in writing |
| Residence commission | Approach 3–5 Madrid student residences | One referral agreement with an agreed commission |
| Settled package | Fake-door test: real price shown to students and parents, with a waitlist or refundable deposit | Pre-orders or deposits, not survey answers |

## Open questions

- Does a university contract make us look less independent to students? Would students trust a university-branded tool more or less?
- Will landlords in a tight market pay for anything, when they can already rent without us?
- Which party should pay for verification so that it stays credible? (If landlords pay, we must be able to refuse or remove the badge.)
